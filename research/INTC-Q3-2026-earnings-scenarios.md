| What makes it go up? | Q4 revenue guide midpoint **≥ $17.5B** (consensus $17.06B), a GM guide of 43% or more, and Q3 GM of 42.5% or more | |

The beat itself is close to expected. In July, Intel beat revenue by about 12%, doubled EPS consensus and guided above the Street. The stock still **closed −7.9% the next day**. A Q3 beat alone does not move this stock. The forward guide and the foundry story do.

**Why the bar sits above consensus:**

1. **Intel beats by design.** Q1 revenue was $13.58B against $12.36B consensus (+9.9%). Q2 was $16.1B against about $14.4B (+11.8%) and its seventh straight beat. The $16.47B Q3 consensus is only about $170M above the guide midpoint, so the Street has not modelled the usual beat. Buy-side expectations ("whisper") are almost certainly higher, around $16.8–17.0B and $0.43–0.45.
2. **The stock has already run.** INTC rose +9.9% in three sessions (from $86.96 on Oct 23 to $95.53), and SMH rose +7.7% over Oct 26–28 (from `market-history.json`). Part of the beat is already in the price.
3. **The July precedent.** A big beat and a raised guide were sold because foundry external revenue was only $293M and AI-capex durability was questioned. The market wants proof about 2027–28, not about Q3.
4. **Valuation leaves little room.** At $95.53 the stock trades at about 60× run-rate EPS ($0.40 × 4) and about 47× a quoted 2027 consensus of $2.04 (sources differ). It is priced on a multi-year ramp, so the guide and trajectory matter more than the quarter.
5. **Estimates are already rising.** About 30 upward EPS revisions and none downward over the last 30 days mean the bar has been moving up into the print.

## 3. Scenarios

EPS comes from a simple sensitivity model built on the guide (see §5): about **+$0.07 per extra $1B of revenue** at 42% GM, and about **+$0.03 per 100 bp of gross margin**.

### Bull, 25%: "Fourth straight blowout." Stock **+10% to +18% → $105–113**

- Q3 revenue **≥ $17.3B**. Supply arrives earlier in the quarter than guided, and server CPU price increases hold.
- GM **≥ 43%**. Xeon makes up more of the mix and pricing outweighs memory and 18A ramp costs. EPS **about $0.50**.
- **Q4 guide ≥ $18B midpoint** (top of the Street range) and GM guide ≥ 43.5%. Management says demand exceeds supply into 2027.
- Foundry: a named external 14A commitment, clarity on Terafab, or an operating loss that narrows toward −$1.8B.
- Ceiling reference: the July after-hours high was $113.55. Above that, late September was about $121.

### Base, 50%: "Beat, held back by supply; guide in line to slightly above." Stock **−3% to +5% → $93–100**

- Q3 revenue **$16.8–17.0B** (+2–3% vs consensus), smaller than the last two beats because output limits sales.
- GM **42.3–42.7%**, EPS **$0.43–0.45**.
- DCAI up QoQ on server pricing. CCG flat to down QoQ: PC units fell 20–22% YoY in Q3, partly offset by higher ASPs.
- **Q4 guide midpoint $17.0–17.5B**, GM about 42.5–43%. The foundry loss stays around −$2.0–2.3B and external revenue grows slowly.
- Reaction depends on the call. The pre-Q2 close ($100.10) is the resistance level and the $95 August offering price is the pivot.

### Bear, 25%: "Supply wall plus margin stall." Stock **−9% to −16% → $80–87**

- Q3 revenue **$16.3–16.6B** (at or just above the midpoint), GM **≤ 41.5%** on memory costs and 18A start-up costs. EPS **$0.36–0.39** (a miss).
- **Q4 guide ≤ $16.8B midpoint.** Substrate and memory shortages, PC weakness, or fallout from the Kiryat Gat fab incident (Oct 15) would land in Q4.
- Capex raised again with no free-cash-flow timeline, and no foundry customer progress.
- **Even a Q3 beat lands here if the Q4 guide comes in under about $17B.**
- Support levels: $86.96 (Oct 23 low), JPM's $85 target, and the mid-$80s late-August low. The January 2026 reaction was about −17%.

**Probability-weighted move:** 0.25×(+14%) + 0.50×(+1%) + 0.25×(−12.5%) ≈ **+1%**. That is close to what options already price. There is no strong edge in positioning before the print. The edge comes from reading the Q4 guide quickly.

## 4. Why I expect a Q3 beat, and what could cap it

**Tailwinds**
- **Server CPU shortage and pricing power.** Agentic AI is pulling CPU:GPU ratios back toward parity. Lead times are 25–30 weeks, and Intel reportedly meets only about half of demand. Server CPU prices are up 10–20%+ since March, with another 8–10% expected in H2. DCAI grew +59% YoY in Q2 at about a 40% operating margin.
- **Mix shift.** Intel is moving wafers from client chips to Xeon, which carries higher margins.
- **Industry read-through.** TSMC's Q3 (Oct 15) beat its own guide, it raised full-year growth to about 44%, and it said the demand-supply gap is "still quite big."
- **Conservative guidance.** Two straight quarters beat the guide midpoint by more than $1B.

**Headwinds**
- **Supply limits the upside.** The Q3 guide is only +1.2% QoQ, and management said supply growth skews to *late* Q3 and Q4. That suggests a smaller Q3 beat and a stronger Q4 guide.
- **PC collapse.** Q3 PC units fell 22.4% YoY per Gartner and 20.1% per IDC, as DRAM costs were passed through to buyers. Client is about 55% of Intel's revenue.
- **Margin pressure.** The CFO called memory costs "growing headwinds in the second half," and the 18A (Panther Lake) ramp brings early-yield costs.
- **Foundry burn.** Q2 operating loss was −$2.1B on $5.8B of revenue, of which only $293M was external. 2026 capex was raised to $20B, and 2027 is set to rise "significantly."

## 5. Model and accounting notes

**EPS sensitivity.** Opex is held fixed, the non-GAAP tax rate is 12%, and the share count is about 5.24B (Q2 non-GAAP net income of about $2.2B divided by $0.42).

| Revenue \ GM | 42.0% | 42.5% | 43.0% |
|---|---|---|---|
| EPS $0.40 needs | $16.58B | $16.39B | $16.20B |
| EPS $0.45 needs | $17.29B | $17.09B | $16.89B |
| EPS $0.48 needs | $17.72B | $17.51B | $17.31B |

This model is conservative. In Q2, Intel actually earned **+$0.12 EPS per $1B** of revenue beat, including the margin upside.

**Share count.** In August Intel sold $20B of stock (210.5M shares at $95, about 4% dilution). That came after the Q3 guide. Interest on the roughly $19.7B raised about offsets the dilution in Q3. It also means each dollar of revenue beat produces about 4% less EPS than it did in Q2.

**Ignore the GAAP headline.** GAAP EPS swings with the mark-to-market on escrowed shares from the US government deal, which was a **$12.5B non-cash charge in Q2** (GAAP EPS −$2.16 vs +$0.42 non-GAAP). Its sign in Q3 depends on the late-September price versus late June. Trade on the non-GAAP line, because algorithms can whipsaw on GAAP in the first minutes.

**Options.** The implied move is **±8.3% (Bloomberg) to ±9.4% (TipRanks)**, which is ±$8–9 or about **$86.50–$104.50**. INTC has beaten its implied move in only 3 of its last 8 reports. The last four reactions ranged from −17.0% to +23.6%.
